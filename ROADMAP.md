# Netrunner Workspace Roadmap

**The single source of truth for status.** `AGENTS.md` is the rules of engagement, `ARCHITECTURE.md` the shape of the engine. This file is the index: where each area stands, what is next, and which area roadmap under `docs/roadmap/` holds the record. **Open an area roadmap only when working in that area** — each is the full log of decisions and measurements for its phase, and none is needed to orient.

Code and docs address entries as `ROADMAP <phase> §<n>` — "Phase 2 §5", "Rules Audit T8", "Phase 1 §8 Stage 5", "Phase 1.75 §6". Those addresses are permanent and resolve through the table below; an area roadmap keeps its section numbers even when an item is compressed to a line.

**Current goal:** a solid single-player Netrunner, then network play.

**Health (10 September 2026):** `cargo test --workspace` green (about 1,200 tests), `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets` silent, both 256-seed sweeps clean under the rules-coverage gate, the card-conservation invariant and the fog gate, with **no stall tolerance** — every seating reaches `GameOver`. `ActionSpace::SIZE` 1646, `OBS_SIZE` 2262, `CARD_VOCAB` 192, `MAX_STEPS` 2,500, `DECISION_BUDGET` 256. **The shipped bots are complete and carry no data**: `Random`, `Heuristic` with its six `Personality` profiles, `Mcts` and `Puct` are pure code — `eval::Weights` constants compiled into `netrunner_bots`, no file read at run time. The one bot that consumes a file, `puct-onnx`, sits behind an off-by-default feature, and **no trained network has yet beaten the `Puct` search it would replace** as a whole: four volume runs, none promoted. **Its two halves have now come apart** (§5 item 22, 8 September 2026) — a checkpoint trained on this engine scores **0.617 priors-only**, over the 0.55 threshold and above baseline on both chairs, against **0.141 value-only**, and seating both gives 0.359. **The fifth run then seated the priors alone and found they do not compound** (§5 item 26): over 1,152 arena games the candidate is −0.8σ from parity. **The "seat trade" read off that run's chair split is withdrawn** (§5 item 32, 10 September 2026): a chair's null is the pool's Corp win rate at that configuration — measured **0.6823 / 0.3177** — not 0.500, so the ±0.22 chair deltas are +0.026 and −0.050 and the candidate is at parity on both chairs. The per-chair promotion floor built on that reading (§5 item 28) was centred on 0.500 and unreachable, and **§5 item 33 re-centres it on a null leg measured per incumbent per arena shape** (`--arena-null`), which restores the gate's ability to promote without changing a single recorded verdict; the `(chair, outcome)` sample weight (§5 item 29) is a small real gain, cb05 being the one leg at or above its null on both chairs. **§5 item 34 then closed the question those nulls left open** (10 September 2026): the pool's Corp share is a function of *search budget* — 0.549 at 32 simulations, 0.586 at 128, **0.703 at 512** with the uniform evaluator and no network anywhere in the process — so seating a policy head never moved the pool; playing like a deeper search did. The Corp chair converts that budget and the Runner chair does not, and `mcts@128` beats `puct@128` on the Runner chair by 0.077 (2.95σ) while losing the Corp chair by 0.090 — making **PUCT's Runner chair the binding constraint on every chair number recorded here**. **§5 item 35 then found what that chair is short of, and it is not depth** (10 September 2026): MCTS's whole lead is its root-parallel tree count — 4 × 32, read off the core count — and at one tree the two searches are within 0.003. Four *different* samples of the hidden state are worth **+0.125** on that chair at identical depth, four copies of one sample only +0.031 (n.s.), and depth past 64 nothing at all, while the Corp chair converts depth (+0.208 over 16 → 128) and not samples. The same knob on PUCT is **−0.102**, landing on `puct@32`'s number. **§5 item 36 then found why, and the answer is structural** (10 September 2026): `evaluate_state_with` is bit-identical across all 16 determinizations in every one of 384 Corp positions and unanimous on the root argmax for the Runner too — PUCT at `samples` 4 averages four identical numbers — while at `mcts`'s 16-ply leaf the sample is worth **+0.070 (z = +7.74)** of argmax movement on the Runner chair against **+0.007 (n.s.)** on the Corp's. **§5 items 37 and 38 then fixed the prerequisite and closed the question** (11 September 2026): every unrezzed ICE in a sample was a `Barrier` with no subroutines and nothing ever repaired it, so item 36's 16-ply sensitivity was sampled HQ and R&D and never the ICE; and the leaf term that finally reads the sampled ICE moves the depth-0 column (+0.032, z = +4.49) while buying **nothing** in games (+0.016/+0.010 on two seeds, below the 0.026–0.047 band), shipping at weight 0.0. The retired runs and their checkpoints are under `data/runs/`; the corpora are deleted.

## What is next, in order

1. **Price the Corp's rez decision, not the Runner's leaf** (Phase 2 §5 item 38). Item 1's old form is closed and the answer was no. The Runner's leaf can now read the sampled ICE (`UNREZZED_THREAT_WEIGHT`, shipped at 0.0 with the apparatus in place), and reading it is worth +0.016/+0.010 — below the band — while a one-ply Runner is hurt 0.05 to 0.06 by it. The term assumes an unrezzed ICE is certain to be rezzed whenever the Corp can afford it, and a real Corp declines; **what is missing is a probability, not a reading**, and a static evaluator has no way to know one. Two routes, and the first is cheap: measure how often the Corp actually rezzes an affordable ICE when approached (a `GameEvent` tally over an existing `bench` run, no new code path), and if that rate is far from 1.0, discount the term by it and re-run the same two-seed legs — `target/diag/str-*.json` and `s2-*.json` are the baselines. If it still buys nothing, the Runner chair is not leaf-bound and item 35's +0.125 for MCTS came from elsewhere — which item 37 already makes likely, since MCTS's samples differ in HQ and R&D contents and never differed in what was behind the ICE.

2. **Should `mcts`'s tree count stop being read off the core count?** (Phase 2 §5 item 35.) `MctsAgent::with_iterations` takes `rayon::current_num_threads().clamp(1, 4)`, so the shipped bot is weaker on a 2-core box on the Runner chair and every `bench` run was reproducible only by accident. `--determinizations` now pins it for a measurement, but the default is still machine-dependent. Deciding the number is cheap; it is listed because it is a *shipped-bot* change and wants the strength bar rather than a quiet edit.

3. **Deeper batching, if a run ever wants it** (Phase 2 §5 item 27). Cross-game batching landed at 2.6× with a byte-identical corpus, and the measured ceiling is higher still — 4 runners at batch 32 reach 46,229 rows/s against the ~11,300 the loop now achieves — but reaching it needs more rows outstanding than ~64 game threads produce. Leaf parallelization inside a search (virtual loss) would supply them and **does** change search results, so it needs the full strength bar. Not worth it until a run is actually waiting on compute.

4. **Whether the pool is Corp-favoured at all, once the Runner chair is fixed** (Phase 2 §5 item 34). 0.703 is an upper bound measured with a Runner we can already beat, and the honest reading of the curve is that it prices *this search*, not the decks. Re-run the budget curve after item 1 lands; if the Corp share falls with a better Runner, there is no balance problem to answer. `scripts/corp_share_curve.py` and `bench` self-pairings are the whole apparatus — 10 s a heuristic leg, 6 min a `puct@128` leg — so this is a re-measurement, not a project.

5. Small open items, each recorded in its area file: `netrunner_gym` can still toggle-loop; the coverage card gate is inert at default seeds for decks the sweep has not played eight times; `netrunner_single_player/tests/common/mod.rs` still carries a filler fixture; Core Set cards have no sweep coverage.

## How this file is kept

- Status lives here or in an area roadmap, never in `AGENTS.md`. A new entry goes in the area file under its numbered section; then update the table row and the "next" list here.
- An entry records the decision, the alternative rejected, and the load-bearing numbers (before → after, seeds, game counts, branch). Narrative is compressed at the next pass; numbers and decisions are not.
- A later entry that corrects an earlier one says so in both places rather than deleting the earlier text.

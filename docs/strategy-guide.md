# Strategy Guide

Learn to Play teaches the rules. This guide is about what to do with them: how a game of Netrunner tends to unfold, what each side is trying to achieve at each stage, and the plans the factions and deck styles are built around. Every card named here is one you can build a deck with in this client.

None of this is a rule. Good players break every piece of advice below when the board says to, and the board always wins the argument.

## The fundamentals

### Clicks, credits and cards

Everything in Netrunner is paid for with three currencies. The Corp gets 3 clicks a turn and a free draw; the Runner gets 4 clicks and no free draw. A click can always be turned into 1 credit or 1 card with a basic action, so a click is worth at least one of either, and a card or an ability is only good if it beats that rate.

- [Hedge Fund](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30075) costs a click and 5 credits and gives back 9: four credits for one click, where clicking for credits would give one.
- [Sure Gamble](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30030) is the Runner's version, and the reason a Runner hand with no money in it is usually worth a mulligan.
- A card that pays out over several turns, like [Nico Campaign](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30037) or [Telework Contract](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30027), costs you now and pays you later. It is worth more the longer the game will last.

### Tempo

Tempo is how quickly you move the game toward the position you want, while keeping your opponent from reaching theirs. Every click should serve next turn's plan as well as this one. A Runner who spends a turn installing a breaker for ICE the Corp has not rezzed has lost tempo; a Corp that spends a turn and all its credits rezzing ICE the Runner could not have got through anyway has lost it too.

Plan the whole turn before you take the first click. If you are going to draw, draw first, so the new card can change the rest of the plan.

### Economy is the war

Most games are decided by credits. The Corp needs them to rez ICE, advance agendas and pay for its operations; the Runner needs them to break that ICE and to trash what they find. A player with a larger, steadier income can force outcomes: they can afford the expensive run, or the rez that stops it. Watch your opponent's credit total as closely as your own. What they can afford next turn tells you what they can do next turn.

### How the game is won

- **Agenda points.** A player with 7 or more agenda points wins (CR 1.7.2a). The Corp scores agendas by advancing them; the Runner steals them by accessing them.
- **Flatline.** The Corp wins if the Runner suffers more damage than they have cards in their grip (CR 1.7.2b). A grip of zero cards is not a loss by itself — the next point of damage is.
- **Decking.** The Runner wins if the Corp must draw from an empty R&D (CR 1.7.2c). An empty stack does not make the Runner lose. It matters in long games, and more against a Corp that draws extra cards.

### Agenda density

Roughly one card in five in a Corp deck is an agenda. That is the Runner's baseline chance that any one card they access is an agenda. The Corp's own play moves it:

- **HQ fills up with agendas** when the Corp keeps drawing but is not scoring. The operations and ICE get played; the agendas stay behind in hand, waiting for a safe server. A Corp holding a full hand and scoring nothing is very likely holding agendas.
- **R&D stays near the deck's average**, which is why a Runner who can see several cards a turn there steals steadily.
- **HQ empties of agendas** when the Corp finds a scoring window. The best HQ run is often the turn before the Corp is ready to score, not after.

## How a game moves

A game has an early, a middle and a late stage, but the clock that decides which one you are in is the board, not the turn count: how much ICE is up, how much of each side's economy and tools are installed, and how close each side is to 7 points.

### The early game

The first few turns belong to the Runner more often than not. The Corp's ICE is not installed yet, or not rezzed, and its economy is fragile.

- **The Corp** installs ICE on HQ and R&D, starts its economy, and takes a cheap score if the Runner gives it one. Most Corp opening hands want at least one piece of ICE and at least one way to make money; a hand of agendas and no ICE is the classic reason to mulligan.
- **The Runner** probes. Run R&D and HQ before they are protected; run into unrezzed ICE, because either the Corp spends credits to rez it or the run goes through. Finding breakers and building money come next, but a Runner who only builds lets the Corp build too.

### The middle game

The board fills up. The Corp builds a scoring remote — a server with several pieces of ICE and sometimes an upgrade — and starts pushing agendas through it. The Runner assembles a rig that can answer the three kinds of ICE (a fracter for barriers, a decoder for code gates, a killer for sentries) and the credits to keep using it.

This is where both sides approach 3 to 5 points, and where abilities marked "Threat" begin to switch on. Threat is not a stage of the game: it is a rule. The threat level is the highest score of either player (CR 1.17.1a), and a "Threat N" ability is active only while the threat level is at least N (CR 9.3.6f). [N-Pot](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35064) has two subroutines that end the run only at threat 2 and threat 4, and [Measured Response](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35078) can be played only at threat 4 or more.

### The late game

Both players are one agenda or one turn from winning, and a mistake ends the game. The Corp looks for a way to score its last points — out of the remote, or straight out of HQ in a single turn — or to flatline a Runner who has run out of cards or credits. The Runner goes for the last agenda wherever it is likeliest to be: R&D with extra accesses, HQ when the Corp is holding cards, or the remote the Corp has spent all game building, even if it takes every credit they have.

Late in the game, count before you act. How many credits does the Corp need to score from hand? How much damage could an access do to you, and how many cards do you hold?

## Playing the Corp

### Make scoring windows

The Corp wins by creating a moment when the Runner cannot or will not contest an agenda. There are three common ways.

- **The taxing remote.** Put enough ICE in front of a server that getting through costs the Runner most of their credits. Even if they steal the agenda, a Runner who spent everything cannot run your next one. [Manegarm Skunkworks](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30042) makes every approach to its server cost 2 clicks or 5 credits on top of the ICE.
- **Never advance.** Install a card in a remote and do not advance it. The Runner cannot tell whether it is an agenda, a trap or an asset. Next turn, [Seamless Launch](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30040) places 2 advancement counters on a card you did not install this turn, and one more advance scores a 3-advancement agenda before the Runner has another turn.
- **Score from hand.** A 2-advancement agenda such as [Greenmail](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35070) can be installed and advanced twice in the Corp's three clicks, and scored without ever sitting on the board. With extra clicks, bigger agendas can be scored the same way: [Nanomanagement](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35043) gives 2 clicks, enough to install a 3-advancement agenda and advance it three times in one turn.

Advancing costs a click and a credit for each counter (CR 5.2.6f), and scoring costs no click. Count the credits before you install: an agenda you cannot finish next turn is an agenda sitting in the open for a second turn.

### Bluff with what you install

Every face-down card in a remote asks the Runner a question, and the Corp gets to choose which answer is expensive. An advanced card might be an agenda about to be scored — or it might be [Urtica Cipher](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30045), which does more net damage for every counter on it, or [Clearinghouse](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30061), which turns its counters into meat damage when your turn begins. Advancing a trap sells it as an agenda; leaving an agenda unadvanced sells it as a trap. Put an asset in your scoring server now and then, so that a Runner has to pay the full price to find out.

### ICE: where it goes and in what order

- **Protect the centrals.** A Runner who steals out of R&D or HQ while you are building a remote takes the points and your momentum. One piece of ICE on each of HQ and R&D is the usual start. Archives matters less, until it holds something worth taking.
- **Each new piece costs more.** Installing ICE costs 1 credit for every piece already protecting that server (CR 1.16.6b), so a deep server is expensive for you as well as for the Runner.
- **Order is a choice, not a rule.** A common habit is to put ICE that ends the run on the outside, so the Runner must have the right breaker just to get in, and ICE that punishes on the inside, where it catches a Runner who has spent their credits getting that far. It is a starting point, not a law; players disagree about it, and the Runner's rig should change your mind.
- **Rez late.** An unrezzed piece of ICE costs the Runner nothing to pass, but it also tells them nothing. Rez it when the run matters, not the moment it is approached.

### Keep money coming

Operations like [Hedge Fund](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30075) are fast; assets like [Regolith Mining License](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30071) or [PAD Campaign](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01109) pay over several turns but can be trashed by a Runner who reaches them. An asset the Runner cannot afford to trash is worth protecting; one they can trash for a click is often only worth installing if they have better things to do.

## Playing the Runner

### Pressure early

- **Run the centrals before they are protected.** A first-turn run on R&D or HQ is often free. Even when it finds nothing, it tells you what the Corp is holding and makes them ICE their centrals before building a remote.
- **Make the Corp rez.** Running into unrezzed ICE costs you only the click if the Corp lets you through, and every rez costs the Corp credits it wanted for scoring. A Corp that spends 6 credits on a rez this turn cannot spend them on an agenda next turn. Unknown ICE feels more dangerous than it usually is; a failed run still tells you what the ICE is.
- **Don't run on your last click** against a Corp that punishes runs — Jinteki with its net damage, NBN with its tags. Keep a click to draw back up or to clear a tag afterwards.

### Where to run

- **HQ** when the Corp is holding cards without scoring, and the turn before you expect them to score. A plain HQ run shows one card at random; [Docklands Pass](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30013) and [Jailbreak](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30028) show more.
- **R&D** when you can see several cards there: [The Maker's Eye](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01036), [Conduit](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30024) and [Devadatta Drone](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35031) all add accesses. Steady R&D pressure steals agendas before the Corp even draws them.
- **The remote** when a card there could be an agenda the Corp can score before your next turn. Read the counters on it and the Corp's credits: an unadvanced card is only a threat if the Corp can afford to finish it next turn.

### Build a rig that pays for itself

- **Economy first, then breakers.** Install breakers for the ICE the Corp has actually rezzed rather than for ICE they might have. A breaker that sits unused is memory and credits you could have spent running.
- **Cover all three types eventually.** A fracter for barriers, a decoder for code gates and a killer for sentries. [Mayfly](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30032) breaks anything for one run and is then trashed — a way through before your rig is ready, not instead of one.
- **Know what a break costs.** A breaker's strength boost lasts only while it breaks the current ICE (unless a card like [Gordian Blade](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01043) says otherwise), and breaking a subroutine does not trash the ICE. The next run pays again.

### Trash the Corp's economy

When you access an asset that makes the Corp money or cards, trashing it is usually worth the credits, even if it leaves you short. An economy card left on the table pays the Corp every turn after, and a few turns of it usually cost you more than the trash cost would have.

### Stay alive

- **Keep your grip larger than the damage you could take.** You flatline when you suffer more damage than you have cards in grip, and an access to a trap can hit you before you see it coming.
- **Clear tags.** A tag costs a click and 2 credits to remove (CR 5.2.7g). While you are tagged, the Corp can trash your resources for a click and 2 credits each (CR 5.2.6g), and punishing cards like [Scorched Earth](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01099) switch on.
- **Core damage is permanent.** Each point lowers your maximum hand size for the rest of the game (CR 5.5.3b).

## Corp deck styles

A Corp deck is built around how it makes its scoring windows. Most real decks mix two of these.

### Glacier: the taxing wall

Build ICE on the centrals and one deep remote, then score behind it over several turns because the Runner cannot afford the run. It needs a lot of money and time, and it is weakest early, while its centrals are still thin. Cards that fit it: [Pharos](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30063), [Kessleroid](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35075), [Whitespace](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30074), [Manegarm Skunkworks](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30042), [Mahkota Langit Grid](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35082) and [Government Subsidy](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30064) to pay for it all.

### Fast advance: no window at all

Score agendas in the turn they are installed, so the Runner never has a turn to run them. It needs extra clicks or advancement from cards, and a hand with the right pieces together, so it is hungry for cards and credits and stalls if the Runner keeps HQ under pressure. In this pool, [Nanomanagement](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35043) and [Otto Campaign](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35040) give the clicks, and [Seamless Launch](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30040), [Touch-ups](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35067) and [Key Performance Indicators](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35077) give the counters — most of them for a card installed on an earlier turn, so here fast advance is usually "install one turn, score the next without advancing in between."

### Kill: threat as leverage

Tag the Runner, then punish the tags; or build net damage until a trap finishes them. The Runner has to choose between leaving your agendas alone and taking the risk. It fails against a Runner who keeps a full grip and clears tags every turn, so a kill deck still needs agendas it can score. Tags: [Public Trail](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30057), [Funhouse](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30054), [Ping](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30055). Payoffs: [Scorched Earth](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01099), [Orbital Superiority](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30068), [Retribution](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30065). Net damage: [Snare!](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01070), [Urtica Cipher](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30045), [Neurospike](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30049).

### Mixing them

- **Glacier, then fast advance.** Score the first agendas behind the wall. Once the Runner has spent everything building a rig that beats the wall, score the last points from hand, where that rig is no use.
- **Tag and punish.** Put a card in a remote that the Runner must check. If they don't, you score; if they do, your ICE and cards tag them, and next turn you punish the tags. A Runner who refuses to be tagged has to spend their credits clearing tags instead of running.
- **Traps behind the wall.** Put traps in the deep remote as well as agendas. A Runner who spends 12 credits getting through three pieces of ICE and finds [Snare!](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01070) has lost far more than the damage.

## The Corp factions

- **Haas-Bioroid** is efficiency: extra clicks ([Nanomanagement](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35043), [Otto Campaign](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35040)), bioroid ICE that a Runner can break by spending clicks instead of credits ([Brân 1.0](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30039)), and [Manegarm Skunkworks](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30042) to tax the remote.
- **Jinteki** is uncertainty: traps, net damage and a threat of flatline behind every face-down card. [Urtica Cipher](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30045), [Karunā](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30047) and [Anoetic Void](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30050).
- **NBN** is information and tags: ICE and operations that tag the Runner, and cards that get stronger when they are tagged. [Funhouse](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30054), [Public Trail](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30057), [Predictive Planogram](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30056).
- **Weyland** is money and force: big economy, ICE that grows when it is advanced, and meat damage. [Government Subsidy](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30064), [Ice Wall](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01103), [Pharos](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30063), [Scorched Earth](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01099).

## The Runner factions

### Anarch: tear it down

Anarchs treat the Corp's board as something to dismantle. They trash what they access, damage the Corp's hand and deck, and put up with costs to themselves — taking damage, or trashing their own cards — for a burst of power. Their breakers are cheap to use but often need help against strong ICE. Strongest in the middle and late game; weakest when their self-inflicted costs catch up with them.

- Breakers: [Corroder](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01007), [Cleaver](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30006), [Buzzsaw](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30005), and [Botulus](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30004), a virus that breaks the ICE it sits on.
- Viruses: [Leech](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30008) weakens ICE, [Fermenter](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30007) turns counters into credits.
- Disruption: [Cacophony](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35010) makes the Corp trash cards from HQ and R&D; [Carnivore](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30003) and [Gourmand](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35007) trash what you access.

### Criminal: take their money

Criminals run early and often, and make money by running while the Corp loses it. They punish an exposed HQ, and they get past ICE instead of breaking through it. Strongest early; they stall if the Corp builds a heavy wall before the pressure lands.

- Money from runs: [Account Siphon](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01018), [Transfer of Wealth](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35017), [Clean Getaway](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35014), [Red Team](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30018).
- Getting past ICE: [Tranquilizer](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30017) derezzes the ICE it sits on; [Fransofia Ward](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35021) bypasses ICE against a rich Corp.
- HQ pressure: [Docklands Pass](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30013), [Maintenance Access](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35016).

### Shaper: build the perfect rig

Shapers start slowly and finish strong. They build a rig of breakers that scale, and once it is complete their runs are cheap and exact — above all on R&D. Strongest late; vulnerable to a Corp that scores fast before the rig is ready.

- Breakers that grow with the rig: [Echelon](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30025), [Unity](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30026), [Principia](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35032).
- Rig support: [DZMZ Optimizer](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30022), [Madani](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35028), [GAMEDRAGON™ Pro](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35027).
- R&D access: [Conduit](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/30024), [Devadatta Drone](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/35031), [The Maker's Eye](https://netrunnerdb.com/en/card/01036).

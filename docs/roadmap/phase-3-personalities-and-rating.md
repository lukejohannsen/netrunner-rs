- **A search baseline for the evaluator's blindnesses** (`target/coverage/puct-baseline.json`; `puct@32` self-play over 192 matchups as 192 single-game processes merged): *Measured Response* played once — search finds a one-ply blindness. **Dividends fired 0 times at 3,270 advancements — not a horizon problem**: `advancement_tokens.min(required)` zeroed the over-advanced token at every depth, and no budget finds a move valued at zero. Paid choices 33 accepted / 672 declined.
- **`agenda_counter_weight` 2.0** (`feat/eval-dividends`): an over-advanced token on a Dividends agenda is worth the counters it becomes, and a scored agenda's counters are worth the same (both halves needed or the value evaporates at scoring). Sized to beat a click plus a credit (0.8) and stay far under a point (20). It moves the isolated decision at **512 iterations, not 128**; in real games (heuristic, `puct@32`, `puct@512` over the six relevant decks) the trigger still fired **zero** times — PUCT explores the "advance" child (~2) last against "score" (~40). Necessary, not sufficient; the barrier is now the search.
- **The ambush lever** (`feat/eval-ambush-lever`): Clearinghouse and Urtica Cipher carry `advancement_requirement: Some(0)`, so their tokens were worth **zero** while their damage *is* the token count — Clearinghouse fired 247 times dealing nothing. `ambush_advancement_weight` 1.0, cap 3, recognised **by text** (`damage_grows_with_advancement`, `punishes_access_with_damage` via `for_each_effect`). Five seeds, 192 games each: Corp flatline wins 12/16/20/16/23 → 26/31/31/31/31, total Corp wins up on every seed; `DamageTaken` *falls* (fewer, lethal hits). Random-vs-random byte-identical.
- **`Trap` retuned** (`feat/trap-profile-tuning`): the profile is now **three ambush terms only** (`ambush_weight` 3.0, `ambush_advancement_weight` 2.8, cap 7, `..base`); every generic knob it carried was costing it (`IceRezzed` 841 against 1,375). Trap Corp against the balanced Runner over five seeds: **460 wins against balanced's 439, ahead on all five**. `ambush_advancement_weight` alone is +14; `ambush_weight` alone is −2. **`opponent_grip_weight` measured inert** (460 against 460) and left the profile — find it a user or delete it. Ladder: trap Corp 1569 ± 140 over balanced 1446.
- **The Corp accepted 0 of 332 paid choices at every seed** (`feat/eval-paid-choice`): accepting parks a further prompt (−`UNRESOLVED_DECISION_WEIGHT`) while declining resolves to nothing — arithmetic, not preference. `pending_decision_upside_weight` 1.5 is credited beside a **lower bound** on what resolving the prompt delivers (`pending_decision_upside`, priced over the *worst* eligible target), safe by construction while it stays below the penalty; a **whitelist** recogniser, because a catch-all priced Touch-ups' continuation and produced a 10,000-action toggle stall (view sweep, seed 7). Corp accepts 0 → 11–15 per seed; everything else inside the band. The remaining blocked cards each need a different term: **Anoetic Void** (the Corp has no counterpart to `ACTIVE_RUN_WEIGHT`), **AU Co.** (a prompt into a prompt, unpriceable by the rule), **Phật Gioan** (damage worth nothing at `opponent_grip_weight` 0).

- **The Corp damage term cannot be built at one ply, and that is now measured rather than suspected** (same branch). `opponent_grip_weight` was deleted, not retuned. The diagnosis was its shape — linear priced five cards to four the same as two to one, when all the value of non-lethal damage sits at the bottom of the grip, and a *lethal* hit needs no term at all because the state is already `GameOver` at `WIN_SCORE`. So it was rebuilt as a shortfall below a floor mirroring the Runner's own `GRIP_SHORTFALL_WEIGHT`, and measured **byte-identical to having no term at all across 480 games on all five seeds** — not one decision changed. At 4× the weight, still nothing. The shape was never the problem: a one-ply evaluator ranks *actions*, so a term differentiates only where an action changes what it reads, and **no Corp action in the sample pool makes the Runner's grip smaller**. Neurospike, the one direct-damage Corp operation in the pool with no play requirement, deals damage equal to agenda points scored *that turn* — zero in the general case, so no weight makes it worth 3 credits; Scorched Earth and Measured Response are not in the pool at all; every other damage source fires from card text on access or on a trigger, never as the action being ranked. A damage term needs a lever before it needs a weight.

- **The Runner chair of PUCT: the search was choosing by noise** (`feat/puct-samples`, 6 September 2026). The chair asymmetry in every puct-vs-puct measurement (Corp 0.69 / Runner 0.31 under uniform search, Phase 2 §5 item 20) was not the Corp seat being strong — random-vs-random and heuristic-vs-heuristic both give the Corp 46% — it was the PUCT Runner being **worse than one ply**. Seated against the *fixed* heuristic Corp over the 192 sample matchups at 128 simulations (game i on matchup i, seed 1+i, one process a game): heuristic Runner 0.583, **PUCT Runner 0.219**, random Runner 0.115. Attribution against a fixed opponent is the measurement that made this legible; puct-vs-puct moves both chairs at once and read a later Corp gain of +0.09 as "no change".

  A traced game showed the mechanism directly: `UniformPolicyEvaluator` squashed `evaluate_state`'s *absolute* score at `tanh(x / 100)`, so every candidate of an ordinary Runner turn sat within 0.005 of the others (a credit is 0.4 / 100), the exploration term alone set the visits — **12 to 19 per candidate whatever their values** — and the argmax was a coin flip; once the Runner was two agendas down the whole tree sat in the flat tail at −0.69, where even that spread collapsed five-fold. Everything the Runner did wrong followed: 1,190 jack-outs against the heuristic's 25 (76 of 79 inspected before any ICE was rezzed), runs into ICE-only remotes, 1,405 credit clicks against 76, a third of the heuristic's accesses, 39 flatlines against 26. **Ruled out first, each on 192 games:** search depth 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 (0.208 / 0.208 / 0.214 / 0.219 — not horizon pessimism); four determinizations a decision (0.219 → 0.219 — not single-sample certainty; `PuctConfig::samples` stays at 1 with the result on the field); and the run term removed from the search's leaves (0.219 → **0.083** — `ACTIVE_RUN_WEIGHT` is load-bearing for the search too, which cannot find access value on its own at 128 simulations).

  **The fix is a root-relative leaf value at a decision-sized scale**: `PolicyEvaluator::anchor` / `evaluate_from`, the static evaluator's leaf reported as `tanh((score(leaf) − score(root)) / 5)` — a credit at 0.08, a subroutine at 0.2, a breaker at 0.54, a point of agenda saturated. A network anchors at 0 and is untouched. Scale 2 measured the same as 5 (0.406 / 0.411) and 10 worse (0.349); four samples on top of it nothing (0.406). - **The door, and the mulligan tie** (`feat/puct-runner-door`, 6 September 2026). Both leads above, traced first and measured before anything was built, on the same seating (PUCT Runner vs the fixed heuristic Corp, 192 matchups, 128 simulations, one process a game).

  **The mulligan was a tie-break.** Traced on six games, `KeepHand` and `TakeMulligan` had exactly 64 visits each and the same Q to four places: the Corp's whole turn sits between the mulligan and the first Runner action, nothing in the evaluator reads what the grip holds, and the two subtrees are the same tree. `pick_action` resolved the tie with `Iterator::max_by`, which returns the *last* maximum — `TakeMulligan`, listed second. The heuristic's 95 of 192 is the same tie broken by its jitter. Forcing `KeepHand` on all 192 games measured 0.411 → 0.391, inside the band, so the mulligan is worth nothing either way and the fix is fidelity: a full tie now goes to the earlier candidate, the engine's null option (192 keeps, 3 mulligans where the search does have an opinion).

Rule 6.1.5b confirms the engine's window (the Runner may jack out before approaching even an unprotected server), so there was no rules fix. The mechanism: `ACTIVE_RUN_WEIGHT` is forfeited however the run ends, so the two differ only by what the breach finds, and a single determinized sample resolves that to one card — an agenda one time in four, otherwise nothing or a trash prompt. The one-ply heuristic never sees past the access, keeps the run term through it, and left 25 times. Three shapes measured, in order:

## 2. Rating & Elo Engine — DONE

`netrunner_rating` (`feat/rating-and-bench`): Glicko-2 (Glickman 2013; reproduces the paper's worked example), a `RatingBook` of one `Standing` (a Corp and a Runner `RoleRecord`) per `Track { HumanVsHuman, HumanVsBot, BotBenchmark }` per participant, one sorted JSON file. Glicko-2 over Elo because every consumer has few games and a deviation lets a ladder say `1612 ± 120` honestly. **A rating period of one game**, both updates from pre-game standings so order cannot matter and a bot may play itself. Tracks and roles never mix; `overall()` sorts a ladder only. Persistence belongs to the consumer (`bench --ratings`, the daemon's `--ratings-file`). **A forfeit is a loss**; a stall is unrated. **The idle seat is read too** (`fix/idle-seat-surrender`): `await_seat`'s third `select!` arm takes a `Surrender` at once and checks an out-of-turn `SubmitAction` against that seat's own `legal_actions_for` — the one place the host re-derives legality, because a `PlayerAction` carries no submitter (the first version applied the idle Runner's `KeepHand` as the Corp's mulligan). The grace clock moved onto the session so an applied idle action cannot restart it. Not built: telling the client its new rating; rating decay.

- **The Corp damage term cannot be built at one ply, and that is now measured rather than suspected** (same branch). `opponent_grip_weight` was deleted, not retuned. - **Both leads measured, and one of them dissolves: seed 1 was carrying two claims it could not support** (`diag/breach-budget-and-identity-carry`, 8 September 2026). The two open leads above. Same seating as every number in the three bullets before it — the PUCT Runner against the fixed heuristic Corp, `--all-matchups` over the 192-deck cross product, one pinned binary per variant, `cli_out1`/`cli_out4`/`cli_ident`/`cli_s4` — and this time **seeds 1, 2 and 3 rather than seed 1 alone**, which is what changed the answer. Until this branch a personality reached a bot only through `--corp-personality`/`--runner-personality`, and `--corp-level` threw it away: `LevelSpec.personality` was hard-coded `Balanced` and `make_seat_agent` ignored `setup.personality` whenever a rung was set, so `--corp-level 4 --corp-personality rush` played balanced and said nothing. **Three changes.** `LevelSpec::with_personality` crosses the two axes (a rung is a strength, a style is a bias on the same evaluator, so the ladder's order survives it; only the exact calibration figures are for `Balanced`), and `make_seat_agent` passes the personality through. `DeckFile::style: Option<String>` names a `Personality` — a string because `netrunner_core` is the bottom of the graph and the vocabulary belongs in `netrunner_bots`, which carries `every_embedded_deck_style_is_a_personality_for_its_side` so a typo fails the build; on the deck rather than the identity because *Discretion Advised* and *Brutal Efficiency* share one identity and play differently. **All 28 sample decks are tagged** from their lists (six ambushes → `trap`; Brân/Pharos/Empiricist in threes → `glacier`; three-advancement agendas and thin ICE → `rush`; ten-plus run events → `aggressive`; a program-heavy rig → `cautious`), 13 Corp and 7 Runner with a style, 8 balanced, each with a one-line `description` giving the reason (26 of 28 had none). **Resolution is one rule in one place** — the flag if given, else the deck's style, else balanced — in `Config::personality_for(side, deck)` for the TUI, the starter game and the headless runner, and in `seat_vs_bot` for the daemon (unset `--bot-personality` reads the dealt deck; the style is in the rating id, `bot:heuristic:aggressive`, pinned by a lobby test). `--corp-personality balanced` is how a measurement switches a deck's style off.

  **Measured as a signature, not a strength claim** (heuristic-vs-heuristic, 96 games over `--all-matchups`, seed 1, one pinned binary, every seat `balanced` against every seat in its deck's style): Corp point wins 31 → 36, flatlines 18 → 19, Runner point wins 47 → 41 — the outcome moves +5 to the Corp, at the edge of the ±3 heuristic band and not claimed. The signature is the point: `CardAdvanced` 868 → 910 and `AgendaScored` 153 → 170 with `IceRezzed` 832 → 798 (six rush Corps against five glacier), Corp draw clicks 189 → 111 and credit clicks 955 → 1,065; runs 3,106 → 3,362 and accesses 3,060 → 3,639 (five aggressive Runners), Runner draw clicks 246 → 110, `DamageTaken` 205 → 152, `AgendaStolen` 226 → 210. Random-vs-random over 132 games is byte-identical to the 11 September pinned binary, as it must be. The sweeps seat `HeuristicAgent::new` directly and never read a deck, so they are unchanged.

## 2. Rating & Elo Engine — DONE

`netrunner_rating` (`feat/rating-and-bench`): Glicko-2 (Glickman 2013; reproduces the paper's worked example), a `RatingBook` of one `Standing` (a Corp and a Runner `RoleRecord`) per `Track { HumanVsHuman, HumanVsBot, BotBenchmark }` per participant, one sorted JSON file. Glicko-2 over Elo because every consumer has few games and a deviation lets a ladder say `1612 ± 120` honestly. **A rating period of one game**, both updates from pre-game standings so order cannot matter and a bot may play itself. Tracks and roles never mix; `overall()` sorts a ladder only. Persistence belongs to the consumer (`bench --ratings`, the daemon's `--ratings-file`, and since 13 September local play's `ratings.json` — below). **A forfeit is a loss**; a stall is unrated. **The idle seat is read too** (`fix/idle-seat-surrender`): `await_seat`'s third `select!` arm takes a `Surrender` at once and checks an out-of-turn `SubmitAction` against that seat's own `legal_actions_for` — the one place the host re-derives legality, because a `PlayerAction` carries no submitter (the first version applied the idle Runner's `KeepHand` as the Corp's mulligan). The grace clock moved onto the session so an applied idle action cannot restart it. Not built: telling the *remote* client its new rating; rating decay.

- **Local play is rated, and the ladder says what to try next** (`feat/local-rating`, 13 September 2026). The `HumanVsBot` track was built for "a player's progress through the difficulty tiers" and was reachable only through the daemon; the TUI, which is how a person plays, recorded nothing and showed nothing. `netrunner_cli::ratings` is the track's third owner: `<data dir>/netrunner/ratings.json` beside the saved decks (`--ratings-file`, `NETRUNNER_RATINGS_FILE`), written whole to a sibling and renamed. **The file holds the book and a game log**, because a `RatingBook` keeps one record per participant and role and cannot answer "how have I done against `veteran`?" — the one question a climber asks. A bot is rated as `bot:<rung>` (`Level::rating_id`) when a level was set — one id per rung, not per style, since the rung is the strength claim — and as the daemon's `bot:heuristic[:style]` otherwise, so the two files agree. **A quit is a forfeit from turn 3** (`GameState::turn` counts each side's turn, so both have played once), nothing before it, and a stall is never rated; the starter game is rated like any other. The game-over modal now reads `Rating (Runner): 1662 ± 290 (was 1500) · vs apprentice: 1–0–0 · Next: try operator (3)`, and `netrunner_cli ratings` prints both chairs, the record against every rung and the suggestion.

  **The suggestion is a windowed record, not the Glicko-2 expected score, and that was measured before it shipped.** The first rule read `Glicko2::expected_score` (now public on the crate) between the player and the rung's bot as both stood on the track. Between two participants who only ever play each other, both starting at 1500 ± 350, the last game dominates: W→0.798, WL→0.356, WWL→0.477, WWWL→0.568, WWLL→0.261 — every sequence ending in a loss pointed down and every one ending in a win pointed up. So `suggest` reads the highest rung played (never an unplayed one, which sits at an uninformative 1500 ± 350 and once sent a player who lost one game at `operator` straight to `novice`; nothing played means `operator`), takes the last five results against it, says nothing under three, and moves one rung on a win share ≥ 0.6 / ≤ 0.4, clamped. The rating itself is still Glicko-2 and still what the player watches move.

- **Local play is casual, not rated: the client's rating book is gone and the record stays** (`fix/local-play-is-casual`, 20 September 2026). **This corrects the bullet above**, which put every local game on a `HumanVsBot` track in a file under the player's own data directory and showed the number as "your rating". A rating is a claim to someone else: it means something only between people, and only when somebody other than the rated player keeps it. That file was the player's to edit or delete, so the number certified nothing — and guarding it taxed the one place a take-back matters most, since Phase 7 §4af ended a game's rating on any undo past what the move had taught. Practice against a bot is where a person should be able to undo a misread.

  **What went.** `netrunner_client::ratings` is `record`: `LocalRecord { games }` with no `RatingBook` beside it, `SeatRecord`, `RecordReport` (the game-over lines are `As Runner vs veteran: 3–0–2` and `Next: try elite (5)`, no `Rating (…): 1662 ± 290`), and `netrunner_rating` is no longer a dependency of the client crate. `--unrated`, `StartChoice::rated`, `Intent::ToggleRated`, the terminal's `r` and the desktop's Rated button are gone — there is no unrecorded kind of game to ask for, and the desktop's dev hook passes no record file so an autoplayed game is nobody's. `UNRATED_BY_UNDO`, the `undone` latch in both drivers and the double-press arming in both clients are gone: one press, one label ("Take it back"), and an undone game is recorded like any other. `netrunner_cli ratings`, `--ratings-file` and `NETRUNNER_RATINGS_FILE` are `record`, `--record-file` and `NETRUNNER_RECORD_FILE`; the terminal's Ratings screen is Record, and the desktop's Profile draws the same lines.

  **`Track::HumanVsBot` left the rating crate**, the daemon included: a game against a seated bot is practice wherever it is played, so `netrunner_server` rates only a match whose two seats are people (`SeatedPlayer::rating_id` is `None` for a bot) and a bot daemon given `--ratings-file` never writes it. Rejected: keeping the track server-side for an operator's human-vs-bot ladder — it is the same number with the same meaning, and the principle is that the rating is between people. The style an unpinned daemon bot plays, which a test read off its rating id, is now in its `MatchList` seat name (`heuristic bot, aggressive`), which is also the one place a person sees it. **A retired track must not brick an operator's file**: `RatingBook` deserializes its outer map by name and drops a track it no longer knows, pinned by `a_book_holding_a_retired_track_loads_with_the_kept_tracks_intact`.

  **What stayed, and why it could.** The rung suggestion never read the Glicko-2 numbers — the paragraph above is the measurement that took it off `expected_score` before it shipped — so removing the book changes no ladder behaviour: `suggest`, its window and its thresholds are untouched, and so is every test of them. The log moves to `<data dir>/netrunner/record.json`; when there is no record yet and the old `ratings.json` sits beside it, its `games` are read (its `book` ignored, the file never rewritten), so the rung a player had climbed to survives the rename (`the_ratings_file_this_replaces_is_read_once_and_its_rung_survives`). **A quit from turn 3 is still a loss in the record**: nothing is at stake but the suggestion's honesty, and a record a person can quit out of only ever climbs. `Session`'s `Rewind::{Free, Undo}` and the engine's two classifiers stay exactly as built — the local drivers stopped branching on the kind, and the line is what a rated game between two people will be held to (Phase 4 §5). `bench` and `Track::BotBenchmark` are untouched.

  No engine change, so no sweep or coverage run is owed. **Open:** where a person's standing on a server is shown (Phase 4 §5 stage d); a rated ladder needs an identity first (same entry).

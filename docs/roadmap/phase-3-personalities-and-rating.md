  **What the Corp column says, and it is the finding.** The one-ply Corp evaluator has exactly three levers that read the cards — an ambush's tokens, an installed agenda, ICE in front of one — and they are already `Trap`, `Rush` and `Glacier`. Every other Corp knob is a shared dial, and twisting one either does nothing an action can see (`Tagger`: no Corp action in the pool lands a tag when it resolves — Public Trail's is the Runner's decline, Bigger Picture's sits behind a `PresentChoice` the upside whitelist cannot price, an agenda's arrives with a score that already outweighs it, ICE tags fire in the Runner's run), or is bound by something other than valuation (`Warden`: a 6-cost bioroid at +0.4 still goes unrezzed because the Corp cannot pay for it when approached — the rez is credit-bound, not value-bound), or trades scoring for the behaviour (`Banker`: `CardAdvanced` −10% is where the games went; the pool's assets are cheap and balanced already rezzes 85–88% of them, so the lever's whole ceiling is the last 12–15%). **A fourth Corp style needs a new card-reading lever or a search, not a weight**, and both are parked research. The closest thing to one is `Banker`'s second cut — a Corp that draws and installs wide, distinct on every seed, at a cost of −0.066 win share against a band of 0.047 — recorded here in case that price is ever acceptable.

  **The Runner column's lesson is the knife-edge at initiation.** One ply sees the run term (0.6) when it starts a run and the once-a-turn success term (2.0) only when it completes one, so any credit weight at or above 0.6 stops the Runner running *at all* — 96 losses of 96 — and any below it is balanced with noise. `Builder` earns its name from installs (hardware and resources at three times balanced's rate) at the cost of five games in 96, the same price `Aggressive` paid in flatlines when it shipped; `Wary` is the one term in the evaluator that reads an unrezzed card (items 38–40 built it for the search, where it bought nothing), switched on, and its signature is the intended one on two of three seeds. Five Runner decks retagged: `bowel_movements`, `party_hard`, `prick_thyself` → `builder`; `sabbatical`, `planning_ahead` → `wary`. The sample pool now carries 13 styled Corp decks over three styles and 11 styled Runner decks over four, with 3 + 1 balanced.

## 2. Rating & Elo Engine — DONE

`netrunner_rating` (`feat/rating-and-bench`): Glicko-2 (Glickman 2013; reproduces the paper's worked example), a `RatingBook` of one `Standing` (a Corp and a Runner `RoleRecord`) per `Track { HumanVsHuman, HumanVsBot, BotBenchmark }` per participant, one sorted JSON file. Glicko-2 over Elo because every consumer has few games and a deviation lets a ladder say `1612 ± 120` honestly. **A rating period of one game**, both updates from pre-game standings so order cannot matter and a bot may play itself. Tracks and roles never mix; `overall()` sorts a ladder only. Persistence belongs to the consumer (`bench --ratings`, the daemon's `--ratings-file`, and since 13 September local play's `ratings.json` — below). **A forfeit is a loss**; a stall is unrated. **The idle seat is read too** (`fix/idle-seat-surrender`): `await_seat`'s third `select!` arm takes a `Surrender` at once and checks an out-of-turn `SubmitAction` against that seat's own `legal_actions_for` — the one place the host re-derives legality, because a `PlayerAction` carries no submitter (the first version applied the idle Runner's `KeepHand` as the Corp's mulligan). The grace clock moved onto the session so an applied idle action cannot restart it. Not built: telling the *remote* client its new rating; rating decay.

- Local play was rated on a `HumanVsBot` track with a suggested rung (`feat/local-rating`, 13 September 2026) — **corrected by the next line.**

- **Local play is casual, not rated: the client's rating book is gone and the record stays** (`fix/local-play-is-casual`, 20 September 2026). **This corrects the bullet above**, which put every local game on a `HumanVsBot` track in a file under the player's own data directory and showed the number as "your rating". A rating is a claim to someone else: it means something only between people, and only when somebody other than the rated player keeps it. That file was the player's to edit or delete, so the number certified nothing — and guarding it taxed the one place a take-back matters most, since Phase 7 §4af ended a game's rating on any undo past what the move had taught. Practice against a bot is where a person should be able to undo a misread.

  No engine change, so no sweep or coverage run is owed. **Open:** where a person's standing on a server is shown (Phase 4 §5 stage d); a rated ladder needs an identity first (same entry).
